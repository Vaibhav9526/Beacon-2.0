BEACON — SIH PRESENTATION QUESTIONS AND MODEL ANSWERS
====================================================

Use these answers as speaking notes. Do not memorize every sentence. Lead with the
one-line answer, then add technical detail only if the judge asks for it.


30-SECOND INTRODUCTION
----------------------

BEACON is a human-governed crisis-intelligence platform that converts multilingual
citizen reports into verified, locally actionable incidents. Citizens can report
with text, voice, photo, video and location, or send an SOS. The authority command
centre receives it in real time, sees AI-assisted duplicate and misinformation
analysis, verifies the evidence, assigns responders, and publishes an official
alert or correction. Unverified citizen claims are always kept separate from
official guidance.


1. WHAT PROBLEM ARE YOU SOLVING?

During a disaster, information is fragmented across calls, social media and local
messages. Authorities receive duplicates and misinformation, while citizens cannot
easily distinguish official alerts from unverified claims. BEACON creates one
traceable route from local evidence to verification, response and trusted public
communication.


2. WHAT IS YOUR SOLUTION IN ONE SENTENCE?

BEACON is a multilingual reporting, verification and emergency-response system that
turns citizen evidence into human-approved incidents, assignments and alerts in real
time.


3. WHO ARE THE USERS?

There are three users: citizens who report incidents or request help, authority
admins who verify and publish information, and responders who receive assignments
and update their field status.


4. WHAT MAKES BEACON DIFFERENT FROM A NORMAL DISASTER-REPORTING APP?

Most systems focus on either reporting, alerts or responder management. BEACON
connects all three. Its important differentiators are five explicit trust states,
multilingual evidence preservation, duplicate clustering, privacy-aware AI, audited
human decisions, corrections, SOS dispatch, and a strict visual separation between
unverified claims and official guidance.


5. SHOW US THE COMPLETE USER JOURNEY.

A citizen registers, selects a language, and submits a fresh report with evidence
and location. The backend searches for nearby related incidents using PostGIS,
checks text similarity and media hashes, and runs the AI analysis gateway. The
incident appears instantly on the dashboard through Redis-backed WebSockets. An
admin reviews the original evidence and analysis, verifies it or uses an audited
emergency bypass, assigns a responder, and publishes an alert. The citizen receives
the status or alert in real time. If facts change, the authority publishes a
correction that visibly supersedes the previous alert.


6. WHY IS THE APP MAP-FIRST?

Disaster information is spatial. A map immediately answers: What is near me? Is the
claim official? Where is the nearest hospital or shelter? BEACON combines that map
with a draggable safety sheet so the critical actions—Report and SOS—remain easy to
reach on a phone.


7. HOW DO YOU PREVENT UNVERIFIED INFORMATION FROM CAUSING PANIC?

New reports start as Unverified and never enter the official alert feed
automatically. They can appear only on a separately labelled optional claims layer.
Official alerts use a shield label and a separate data path. Only an authorized
admin can verify or publish an incident.


8. WHAT ARE YOUR TRUST STATES?

Unverified means not yet confirmed. Corroborated means supported by multiple related
reports but not officially approved. Verified means approved by an authority.
Misleading means contradicted or deceptive. Outdated means the information was once
relevant but should no longer guide current action.


9. DOES AI DECIDE WHAT IS TRUE?

No. AI is a decision-support layer, not the source of official truth. It summarizes,
translates, detects duplicate signals, highlights possible misinformation indicators
and recommends a trust state. An authorized human makes the official decision. The
public is never shown an AI confidence value as a “truth percentage.”


10. WHERE IS THE “AI BRAIN” IN THE PRODUCT?

Every report produces a stored AnalysisRun. The gateway records the provider,
latency, validated output, confidence, errors, fallback path and redactions. The
dashboard exposes specialist views for linguistic analysis, duplicate/radar signals,
media evidence and recommended action, together with provenance so the operator can
understand why a suggestion was made.


11. WHICH AI MODELS DO YOU USE?

The gateway tries Gemini first, Groq second, and then a deterministic local analyzer.
The prototype currently remains fully demonstrable without cloud keys because the
local analyzer handles normalization, duplicate evidence, risk signals and approved
protocol selection. Adding configured provider keys activates the cloud stages
without changing the application workflow.


12. WHAT HAPPENS IF EVERY AI PROVIDER FAILS?

The report is retained as Unverified, analysis is marked unavailable, and it remains
visible for human review. It is never silently discarded. SOS requests bypass this
analysis dependency and are always delivered to the emergency queue.


13. HOW DO YOU DETECT DUPLICATE REPORTS?

We first narrow candidates by hazard type, time window and geographic distance using
PostGIS ST_DWithin. We then compare normalized multilingual text and SHA-256 media
hashes. Matching reports are attached to one incident while every original report
and media item remains preserved. The default geographic threshold is configurable
and is currently 2.5 kilometres.


14. WHAT IF TWO INCIDENTS ARE NEAR EACH OTHER BUT ARE ACTUALLY DIFFERENT?

Location alone does not cause a merge. Hazard type, time, text similarity and media
evidence are also considered. The authority can review the original submissions and
keep incidents separate; merge/split remains a human-governed operational action.


15. HOW DO YOU DETECT MISINFORMATION?

BEACON looks for contradictions, suspicious wording, duplicate/reused media,
corroborating nearby evidence and recency. These are indicators, not an automatic
verdict. The authority reviews the evidence before marking an incident Misleading.
That decision is audited and any related active alert is corrected or superseded.


16. HOW DO YOU HANDLE MULTILINGUAL REPORTS?

The interface supports English, Hindi and Chhattisgarhi. We preserve the original
language content beside its English translation so an authority can inspect the
source. Voice and translation are adapters, meaning better speech or language models
can be added without changing the incident workflow.


17. WHY INCLUDE CHHATTISGARHI?

Emergency tools fail when citizens must translate themselves under stress. A local
language reduces friction, improves inclusion and preserves the local meaning of a
report. The architecture can add more regional languages through the same adapter
pattern.


18. HOW DOES SOS WORK?

The user must press and hold, which reduces accidental activation. A short
cancellation countdown provides another safeguard. Once confirmed, the SOS appears
in the authority priority queue and can continue receiving location updates until it
is cancelled or resolved. Assignment and responder ETA/status updates return to the
same citizen session.


19. WHY MUST SOS NEVER BE REJECTED BY AI?

AI uncertainty must not become a barrier to emergency assistance. SOS is treated as
an operational request, not as a truth-classification problem. AI may assist the
operator, but it cannot block, delete or downgrade the request.


20. HOW DOES REAL-TIME SYNCHRONIZATION WORK?

Clients connect through authenticated WebSockets. The API publishes domain events
through Redis Pub/Sub, allowing multiple API instances to distribute the same event.
Events are audience-scoped: authorities receive operational incidents, while a
citizen receives public updates and only their own precise SOS/dispatch information.


21. WHY USE REDIS?

Redis supports low-latency Pub/Sub for real-time events, shared rate-limit counters
and future queue/store-and-forward workloads. It also prevents realtime behavior
from being tied to a single API process, which is important when horizontally
scaling.


22. WHY USE POSTGRESQL, POSTGIS AND DRIZZLE?

PostgreSQL gives durable transactional records; PostGIS provides correct spatial
queries for nearby incidents and geofences; Drizzle provides a typed schema and
migrations shared with the TypeScript API. This is more production-shaped than
storing geographic data as unstructured JSON or relying on in-memory state.


23. WHY DID YOU USE FASTIFY INSTEAD OF THE ORIGINAL FASTAPI PLAN?

The active prototype uses Fastify so the Next.js dashboard, Expo client contracts,
Drizzle models and realtime server share one TypeScript ecosystem. Fastify also has
low overhead and strong schema/plugin support. The earlier FastAPI implementation is
retained as a fallback/reference, but the Docker runtime has one clear active API to
avoid split ownership.


24. WHAT DOES DOCKER PROVIDE?

Docker Compose starts four reproducible services: the dashboard, API, PostGIS and
Redis. Health checks verify the dependencies before the demo. Persistent volumes
retain database, Redis and locally stored evidence across ordinary restarts.


25. HOW DO YOU PROTECT PERSONAL DATA BEFORE USING CLOUD AI?

Before any cloud-AI request, BEACON redacts known names, phone numbers, email
addresses and exact coordinate patterns. Precise locations remain in the operational
database and are not included in the public map. The analysis audit records which
redaction classes were applied without exposing the removed value.


26. WHO CAN SEE PRECISE LOCATIONS?

Precise coordinates are stored only where operationally needed—for reports, active
SOS and authorized assignments. Public views receive an approximate area. Realtime
events are audience-scoped so one citizen cannot subscribe to another citizen's SOS
location.


27. HOW IS AUTHENTICATION HANDLED?

The prototype issues expiring device-bound citizen sessions and expiring authority
sessions. Authority passwords are stored using salted scrypt hashes. Protected
mutations require bearer tokens, ownership is checked server-side, and anonymous
WebSocket connections are rejected. The model includes organization, jurisdiction,
role and MFA-ready fields for production onboarding.


28. WHY IS CITIZEN REGISTRATION ALLOWED WITHOUT OTP?

It keeps the live prototype usable when SMS credentials or mobile delivery are not
available. Abuse is reduced with device sessions and Redis rate limits. Production
deployment would add verified OTP or government-approved identity onboarding based
on the deployment policy.


29. WHAT IS THE EMERGENCY “BYPASS VERIFICATION” FEATURE?

It lets a privileged admin publish rapidly when waiting would create greater harm.
It requires an explicit reason and creates an immutable audit event. In the demo it
is scoped to test users. It is not a hidden shortcut and should require stronger
role checks and confirmation in production.


30. HOW DO CORRECTIONS WORK?

The old alert is marked as superseded, a Correction record is linked to it, and a
replacement alert is published. The correction is sent through the same recipient
delivery pipeline and broadcast in real time. Users therefore see that previous
guidance is outdated instead of silently seeing edited history.


31. WHAT IS IN THE AUDIT TRAIL?

Consequential actions—report creation, verification decisions, bypasses, alert
publication, corrections, assignments, facility changes and moderation—record the
actor, action, entity, timestamp, reason and relevant structured detail. This makes
decisions reviewable after an incident.


32. HOW DO MEDIA UPLOADS WORK?

Photo, video and audio evidence are accepted through multipart uploads with file
count and size limits. Files receive SHA-256 hashes for integrity and duplicate
signals. The media adapter uses Cloudinary when all required configuration is
present; otherwise it uses durable local storage so evidence capture still works.


33. IS CLOUDINARY ACTIVE IN THIS DEMO?

The Cloudinary adapter is configured, but the current restricted API key does not
have the provider's media “create” permission. BEACON therefore records the failed
cloud attempt and safely stores the evidence in its persistent local volume. Once an
upload-enabled key is supplied, the same adapter uses Cloudinary without an app-code
change. Secrets stay in an ignored environment file and are never embedded in the
mobile app or tracked source files.


34. WHAT HAPPENS DURING POOR OR NO CONNECTIVITY?

The mobile app caches the latest safety context and clearly timestamps cached
information. Reports can enter an offline outbox and retry when connectivity
returns. Server delivery follows in-app realtime, configured push, configured SMS,
then queued store-and-forward. The current prototype does not claim peer-to-peer
mesh networking.


35. WHAT IS REAL DATA AND WHAT IS DEMO DATA?

Maps come from OpenStreetMap and weather comes from Open-Meteo. The incident shown
during judging is created live rather than pre-seeded. Authority accounts and
facilities are local demo records. No live government emergency feed or direct 112
integration is claimed.


36. WHY OPENSTREETMAP AND OPEN-METEO?

They provide real geographic and current weather context without requiring paid keys
for the prototype. Both are isolated behind application services so an authorized
government map, weather or routing provider can replace them later.


37. HOW DO YOU PRIORITIZE THE AUTHORITY QUEUE?

SOS requests are presented as the highest operational priority. Incidents are then
ordered by severity, trust/review need and recency. This prevents the command centre
from behaving like a simple chronological inbox.


38. HOW DO COMMUNITIES HELP?

An authority can create or approve an incident-area community for localized updates
and mutual help. Official messages are visually labelled, precise citizen locations
are protected, users may opt out, and authority moderation actions are audited.


39. CAN AI GENERATE MEDICAL ADVICE?

No. BEACON uses an approved static safety-protocol library. AI may select a relevant
protocol, but it cannot invent diagnoses, medication or treatment. This keeps safety
guidance bounded and reviewable.


40. HOW IS THE SYSTEM ACCESSIBLE?

The mobile design uses large touch targets, screen-reader labels, scalable text,
plain-language status text and non-color indicators. The dashboard supports keyboard
navigation, visible focus, focus-trapped dialogs and reduced-motion behavior.
Critical trust states are communicated with text and icons, not color alone.


41. HOW WILL THIS SCALE?

The API is stateless apart from PostgreSQL and Redis, so multiple API containers can
run behind a load balancer. PostGIS spatial indexes can narrow nearby searches,
Cloudinary/object storage can serve media, and background workers can process AI and
delivery queues. Jurisdiction partitioning and retention policies would be added for
multi-district deployment.


42. WHAT IS YOUR DEPLOYMENT PLAN?

Phase one is a controlled pilot with one district authority, trained operators and
test recipients. Phase two integrates approved SMS/push providers and government
feeds. Phase three introduces multi-jurisdiction tenancy, formal MFA, disaster
recovery, observability, data-retention controls and audited production onboarding.


43. WHAT IS THE BUSINESS OR ADOPTION MODEL?

BEACON is best positioned as government or institutional infrastructure rather than
a consumer subscription. A state, district, municipal authority, campus or NGO can
operate it as a managed deployment. Costs are driven mainly by hosting, media,
messages and optional AI usage; open map/weather sources and the local AI fallback
keep pilot costs low.


44. WHAT KPIs WOULD YOU MEASURE IN A PILOT?

We would measure report-to-acknowledgement time, verification time, duplicate
reduction, SOS assignment time, correction delivery success, alert reach, offline
queue recovery, false merge/split rate, operator workload, and citizen comprehension
of official versus unverified information. We would not claim lives saved without a
proper field study.


45. HOW WOULD YOU VALIDATE THE AI?

Create a labelled multilingual dataset with disaster-management experts, including
duplicates, unrelated nearby events, misinformation, outdated reports and noisy
media. Evaluate recall and precision for duplicate suggestions and risk indicators
separately by language and hazard. Human overrides and corrections become feedback
for evaluation, but production model updates require review rather than automatic
self-training.


46. WHAT ARE THE BIGGEST RISKS?

The largest risks are malicious reports, operator overload, location privacy,
provider outages and public over-trust in AI. Our controls are rate limits, evidence
retention, clustering, role-based human approval, approximate public locations,
provider fallbacks, corrections and explicit trust labels.


47. WHAT ATTACKS HAVE YOU CONSIDERED?

We considered identity spoofing, cross-citizen data leakage, anonymous realtime
subscriptions, report flooding, oversized uploads and secret leakage. The prototype
uses server-derived identity, ownership checks, authenticated audience-scoped
WebSockets, Redis rate limits, upload limits, hashed authority passwords and log
redaction. A production security review and penetration test would still be required.


48. WHAT HAVE YOU ACTUALLY TESTED?

The mobile TypeScript check and Expo diagnostics pass, the Next.js production build
passes, and API unit tests validate PII redaction and deterministic AI behavior. The
integration smoke test verifies PostgreSQL/PostGIS, Redis, the AI fallback, media
storage and 16 scoped realtime events. A separate smoke test submits a citizen report,
receives its WebSocket event and confirms it appears in the authenticated authority
queue. The Android UI and live OpenStreetMap were also checked on an emulator.


49. WHAT IS NOT YET PRODUCTION-READY?

Direct 112 integration, formal MFA/onboarding, government data contracts, production
push/SMS credentials, peer-to-peer Bluetooth/Wi-Fi mesh, nationwide jurisdiction
management, formal security certification, load testing and field validation are
not part of this judge prototype. We state these limits clearly rather than
simulating integrations we do not have.


50. WHY SHOULD THIS PROJECT WIN?

BEACON addresses the hardest part of crisis communication: turning noisy local
reports into accountable action without pretending AI is the authority. The
prototype demonstrates the whole loop on real devices and real infrastructure—map,
multimedia evidence, spatial clustering, AI provenance, human verification,
dispatch, alerts, corrections and realtime synchronization—with practical fallback
behavior when optional services fail.


DIFFICULT FOLLOW-UP QUESTIONS
-----------------------------

51. “ISN'T THIS JUST ANOTHER CRUD DASHBOARD?”

No. The value is in the stateful trust and response pipeline: spatial clustering of
independent evidence, persisted AI provenance, human-controlled verification,
audited override, audience-scoped realtime delivery, SOS ownership and correction
propagation. The dashboard is the operational interface to that pipeline.


52. “WHAT IF TEN PEOPLE COPY THE SAME FALSE MESSAGE?”

Volume alone does not make a report Verified. Multiple reports can raise it only to
Corroborated, and copied text or matching media is visible as duplicate evidence.
Official verification still requires an authorized human and supporting evidence.


53. “WHAT IF AN AUTHORITY MISUSES THE BYPASS?”

The action requires privilege, explicit confirmation and a reason, and it produces
an immutable audit entry. Production policy can require dual approval for high-impact
alerts and trigger an independent review of every bypass.


54. “WHY NOT USE BLOCKCHAIN FOR THE AUDIT LOG?”

The immediate requirement is append-only accountability with controlled access,
transactional consistency and reliable operations. A conventional audited database
is simpler and easier for authorities to operate. Cryptographic signing or an
external immutable archive can be added if policy requires it; blockchain is not
necessary to prove the product workflow.


55. “HOW DO YOU KNOW A PHOTO IS FROM THE REPORTED LOCATION?”

We do not claim that a photo alone proves location. We preserve upload metadata and
hashes, compare it with nearby independent reports, and allow field responders to
corroborate conditions. Future production work could add capture attestation and
device integrity checks, but human verification remains necessary.


56. “WHY NOT SIMPLY USE WHATSAPP GROUPS?”

Messaging groups are useful for communication but do not provide structured trust
states, geospatial incident clustering, responder assignments, corrections to all
prior recipients, protected operational locations or a formal audit trail. BEACON
can complement familiar messaging channels through delivery adapters.


57. “WHAT HAPPENS IF REDIS GOES DOWN?”

Redis realtime and shared rate limiting become unavailable, but PostgreSQL remains
the source of durable operational truth. Clients reconcile through REST and queued
delivery can resume when Redis returns. Production would use managed Redis with
replication and monitoring.


58. “WHAT HAPPENS IF POSTGRESQL GOES DOWN?”

The health endpoint fails readiness so traffic should not be routed to that API
instance. The mobile outbox retains new non-SOS reports for retry. Production needs
database replication, backups, point-in-time recovery and a documented emergency
degraded-mode procedure.


59. “CAN THIS WORK IN A VILLAGE WITH WEAK INTERNET?”

The current app caches safety packs and queues reports for reconnection, and the
delivery architecture supports configured SMS fallback. True no-network operation
between devices would require the deferred Bluetooth/Wi-Fi Direct mesh adapter. We
do not claim that capability in this prototype.


60. “HOW WILL YOU AVOID VENDOR LOCK-IN?”

Maps, weather, AI, media and message delivery are behind adapters. Gemini can fall
back to Groq and local analysis; Cloudinary can fall back to local/object storage;
and delivery can move from WebSockets to push, SMS or queues. Core incidents,
evidence and audit data remain in PostgreSQL/PostGIS.


LIVE DEMO NARRATION
-------------------

1. “There is no pre-seeded disaster. I am creating a live citizen report now.”
2. Show the mobile map, current weather, facilities and official/unverified legend.
3. Submit text plus media/location, or activate the hold-to-SOS flow.
4. “The command centre received the event through authenticated Redis-backed
   WebSockets; this is not a manual refresh.”
5. Open the incident and point to original evidence, duplicate signals, provider,
   fallback path and AI redactions.
6. “AI recommends; it cannot publish. I am verifying this as the authority.”
7. Assign the responder and update the status/ETA.
8. Publish an official alert and show it arrive on the citizen app.
9. Publish a correction and show the old alert visibly superseded.
10. Open the audit trail and show the actor, action, reason and timestamp.


IF SOMETHING FAILS DURING THE DEMO
----------------------------------

Map unavailable:
“The operational workflow does not depend on map tiles. Coordinates and PostGIS
queries still work, while the UI shows cached context and allows a manual pin.”

Cloud AI unavailable:
“The provider failure is recorded, PII stays protected, and the deterministic local
analyzer continues. The report remains human-reviewable.”

Realtime temporarily disconnected:
“The connection state is explicit. PostgreSQL remains the source of truth and the
client reconciles through REST while the socket reconnects.”

Media provider unavailable:
“The adapter records the Cloudinary failure and uses durable local storage, so the
evidence is not lost.”

Internet unavailable:
“The local Docker network still demonstrates report, verification, dispatch and
alerts. The citizen app uses cached safety context and queues reports for retry.”


FINAL CLOSING LINE
------------------

BEACON does not ask citizens to trust an algorithm. It helps authorities turn local
evidence into faster, accountable and clearly communicated action—even when
connectivity or optional providers fail.
